Invernadero Comunitario Sangre · CCGS-C1

The People's Greenhouse

El Invernadero del Pueblo

Sustainable. Inexpensive. For the people.

Sostenible. Económico. Para la gente.

Two separate projects, same isomorphism. Project 1 — the community greenhouse: a half-buried structure, earth berm, buried heatpipes, vents, insulation. It needs no power at all. Project 2 — the center: education and a sustainable data center, where the compute lives. Both start simple and step up only as the community's surplus earns each step.

Dos proyectos separados, mismo isomorfismo. Proyecto 1 — el invernadero comunitario: una estructura semienterrada, berma de tierra, tubos de calor enterrados, ventilación, aislamiento. No necesita electricidad. Proyecto 2 — el centro: educación y un centro de datos sostenible, donde vive el cómputo. Ambos empiezan simple y suben solo cuando el excedente de la comunidad gana cada paso.

Solar, wind, and electricity can come in anytime to augment either project — even at Phase 0 — although both work without them.

La solar, la eólica y la electricidad pueden entrar en cualquier momento para aumentar cualquiera de los dos proyectos — incluso en la Fase 0 — aunque ambos funcionan sin ellas.

Electricity flows in. Compute cycles burn. Heat transfers to water. Biology does the rest.

This is not metaphor — this is the acequia, running on electricity. The hot water comes from the center; the greenhouse and the fish are what it sustains.

The ladder · One pattern, two projects

Start simple. Step up as surplus earns each step.

The tiers are isomorphic: the same cell, stepped up. The most common misunderstanding about this project is reading a higher tier's features into the base build. The base greenhouse has no QAP, no controller, and no compute — the compute lives in the center.

Tier 0 · Greenhouse project

Ancient-tech greenhouse

Earth berm, buried earth tubes, black thermal barrels, 50° south glazing, roll-up sides. No power, no fish, no sensors, no controller.

Tier 1 · Greenhouse project

Plus aquaponics

Tier 0 plus the fish loop: fish tank, clarifier, biofilter, algae, chinampa grow beds. Low-tech only. Fish need a permit.

Tier 2 · Center project

Plus compute heat + controller

The center's first step: compute waste heat and the sensor controller / QAP, heating the greenhouse water from the center.

Tier 3 · Center project

The full center

Sustainable community training center and teaching institution; living aquaponics center; large compute heats the water and holds the community database and AI — a closed-loop sustainable data center.

Part 1 · Project 1 — The Community Greenhouse

So we can eat out here.

Food prices are ridiculous. The community greenhouse exists so we can eat out here — and done correctly, we sustain ourselves. Greens first, surplus shared with neighbors. SFCC does good work — they fed people through COVID, running their traditional greenhouse on a huge solar array. Their program is good. But in practice, the commons can afford this design: it needs no power at all. This project has no compute in it. The compute lives in the center, Part 2.

Tier 0 · Passive first

The greenhouse holds its own heat.

A half-buried structure: earth berm, buried heatpipes, thermal barrels, roll-up vents. It needs no power. Sun → 50° glazing → thermal barrels + earth tubes → vents.

Natural heat

The sun and the mass do the heating.

50° south glazing catches maximum winter gain and sheds summer heat. Black thermal barrels along the north wall absorb daytime solar gain and release it through the night. The 500-gallon tank is the largest thermal battery in the building. Deep soil holds 55–60°F year-round.

Natural cool

The earth and the vents do the cooling.

Buried earth tubes — corrugated pipe 6 to 8 feet down — pre-condition incoming air passively, without electricity. Roll-up sides and vents dump the hot air. The steep glazing angle sheds summer heat on its own. A greenhouse here is a wind job first: spring gusts pass 30 mph, and the structure has to respect that.

Underground

Even a mushroom farm.

A certain part of the underground houses a mushroom farm — the buried part stays cool, dark, and humid, which is exactly what mushrooms want. The greenhouse grows food above and below grade.

SYS-AQUAPONICS

Fish + Plant System

Fish waste → biofilter → plant nutrients → clean water return. The grow beds are chinampa-style rafts — Mayan/Aztec floating gardens, not modern plastic. Algae augments the filtration — harvested algae becomes plant food and fish food, and biofuel if there's an abundance. Temperature stability from the thermal loop increases nitrification efficiency. The biology runs cleaner when it is warm and consistent.

SYS-COMPUTE — LIVES IN THE CENTER

Not in this project

The compute rack is not part of the greenhouse. It lives in the center, Part 2, where its waste heat becomes the hot water that sustains this loop. The boundary is the heat exchanger.

Phases · Greenhouse project

Do not build everything at once.

Phase 0 is not production — it is data. You test the structure against the wind, measure the thermal stability of the buried earth system, and learn how your site behaves before you invest serious capital.

Why this is bigger than a greenhouse: the communities stripped of land and water in northern New Mexico do not have to wait for courts or legislatures to give it back. They can build infrastructure right now that makes the dispossession functionally irrelevant. A greenhouse that feeds families and produces fish, sustained by the center's waste heat, is not a political statement. It is a material fact.

Part 2 · Project 2 — The Center

The center: teaches, produces food, very efficient.

This is the second project, not a phase of the first. The compute lives here — in the center, which is the education. Two threads meet here: the community greenhouse, and sustainable data centers — hot water from compute sustaining the greenhouse and the fish.

The Center's math · SPEC-T1 baseline

What the compute adds.

This is the center's math — the data-center thread. It is Tier 2/3 module math, not a property of the Tier 0 greenhouse, which has no tank heater and no compute rack. Every data center on earth is hemorrhaging heat. At 5 kilowatts of compute load, a single rack generates over 17,000 BTUs of heat per hour. That is enough to sustain life.

5 kW compute load × 3,412 BTU/kWh

= 17,060 BTU/hr heat output

500 gal tank × 8.34 lb/gal × 1 BTU/lb°F = 4,170 BTU per °F

17,060 BTU/hr × 0.80 efficiency = 13,648 BTU/hr into the tank

13,648 ÷ 4,170 = ~3.27°F/hr temperature rise

The 5 kW figure is one rack at high utilization. The math was verified against the paper's own arithmetic.

Tier 2 · Center project

Sensor + Control Layer (QAP)

  • State A, Earth: geothermal 12°C constant, earth-tube flow 0–1 (max 0.5 m³/s).
  • State B, Water: 20–25°C tilapia target, pH 6.8, dissolved oxygen 6.5 mg/L, pump 1.2 L/s.
  • State C, Structure: 24-inch adobe wall, thermal capacity 8,000,000 J/°C, 4-hour thermal lag.
  • State D, Air & Compute: ambient target 22°C, compute 2–10 kW, exhaust 35°C.
  • Rule: bang-bang — below target, +1 kW and tubes closed; at or above, −1 kW and tubes open.
  • QAP: 11-byte header + 4-byte float32 payload = 15-byte packets.

Honest status: protocol proven, regulation NOT proven. In the May 2026 sim re-run, water hit 47.5°C at midday — fish would not survive — and the adobe wall never moved, its coupling scaled down ~10,000×. No "stabilized" claim until a re-run holds water in 20–27°C for 24 hours.

Phase 2 · Year 2+

Compute Integration

HPC rack installation, 5–10 kW load, plate heat exchanger to the fish tank thermal loop, NUMARA orchestration layer connected, second bay expansion to 900–1,200 sq ft, geothermal hydronic loop, multi-species zones, fruiting crop section, community dashboard + education programs, full REAP solar funding application.

Target cost: $25,000 – $60,000

The QAP, the controller, and the compute heat are the prototype of the center. The greenhouse scales up to them; it does not start there. The center is the destination, not a footnote.
