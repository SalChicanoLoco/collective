\begin{abstract}

Traditional cup and propeller anemometers rely on mechanical rotation, suffering from severe rotational inertia ($\tau \sim 1\text{--}3\text{ s}$), bearing wear, freeze susceptibility, and an inability to resolve 3D updrafts or high-frequency micro-turbulence. We introduce Magneto-Acoustic Aeolian Anemometry: an array of tensioned ferromagnetic strings vibrating over electromagnetic coil pickups (analogous to electric guitar pickups). Fluid flow triggers Von K\'arm\'an vortex shedding at the Strouhal frequency $f_s = \mathrm{St} \frac{v_\perp}{d}$, exciting aeroelastic string harmonics and inducing an alternating electromotive force (EMF) via Faraday's Law $\mathcal{E}(t) = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$ at kilohertz bandwidth ($> 10\text{ kHz}$). By arranging strings in a tri-axial tripod or tetrahedral configuration, we reconstruct the full 3D wind velocity vector $\vec{v}(t)$ and extract atmospheric turbulence spectra in real time without moving bearings.

\end{abstract}

\vspace{1em}

Introduction: Beyond Mechanical Propellers

\begin{physbox}[Why Mechanical Spinning Propellers are Obsolete]

[leftmargin=*]

  • Inertial Lag: Propeller mass acts as a low-pass filter, blinding the sensor to micro-gusts and acoustic vortex shedding ($> 10\text{ Hz}$).
  • Bearing Degradation: Mechanical bearings freeze in winter, collect dust, and suffer frictional calibration drift.
  • 1D/2D Projection: Vane propellers only align with horizontal flow, missing vertical updrafts, downdrafts, and 3D vortex tilt.

\end{physbox}

\begin{conceptbox}[The Electric Guitar Principle for Wind Sensing]

Tensioned steel/nickel strings mounted over permanent magnets wrapped in fine copper coils generate millivolt signals directly proportional to string velocity:

$

\mathcal{E}(t) = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -N \frac{d}{dt} \int \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{A}

$

When air flows past the string, shedding vortices excite the string modes, converting fluid energy directly into electromagnetic signals at acoustic frequencies with zero moving bearings.

\end{conceptbox}

Vortex Shedding \& Strouhal Law

For cross-flow over a cylindrical string of diameter $d$, vortices shed alternately at frequency:

$

f_s = \mathrm{St} \cdot \frac{v_\perp}{d}, \qquad \mathrm{St} \approx 0.20 \quad (\text{for } \mathrm{Re} \in [300, 10^5])

$

where $v_\perp = \|\vec{v} - (\vec{v} \cdot \hat{s})\hat{s}\|$ is the wind velocity component perpendicular to string unit axis $\hat{s}$.

String Harmonics \& Aeroelastic Lock-In

A string of vibrating length $L$, tension $T$, and linear mass density $\mu$ possesses mechanical natural frequencies:

$

f_n = \frac{n}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}

$

When the vortex shedding frequency $f_s$ approaches an eigenmode $f_n$, lock-in resonance occurs, amplifying string displacement $y(t)$ and output voltage $\mathcal{E}(t)$ by the mechanical quality factor $Q \sim 30\text{--}100$.

Active Self-Calibration: The Closed-Loop EBow Principle

A key challenge in string anemometry is thermal expansion: ambient temperature fluctuations shift string baseline tension $T$ and natural frequencies $f_n$.

\begin{conceptbox}[Closed-Loop Electromagnetic Plink \& Ring-Down Calibration]

Rather than adding external tension sensors, we exploit the pickup coils in dual send/receive mode (analogous to an EBow or Sustainiac driver):

\begin{enumerate}

  • Active Drive Ping: The coil emits a $20\text{--}50\text{ ms}$ magnetic pulse, physically plucking the string.
  • Free Decay Listening: The pickup captures the mechanical ring-down pitch $f_1$.
  • Exact Tension Inversion:

$

T = 4 L^2 \mu f_1^2

$

achieving real-time tension recalibration to $< 0.15\text{ N}$ precision without external strain gauges.

\end{enumerate}

\end{conceptbox}

Multi-Pickup Spatial Harmonic Filtering

By placing three pickups along the string—Bridge ($x = 0.15 L$), Middle ($x = 0.50 L$), and Neck ($x = 0.85 L$)—we perform spatial harmonic comb filtering:

$

y(x_k, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \sin\left(\frac{n \pi x_k}{L}\right) \cos(\omega_n t + \phi_n)

$

Furthermore, the phase lag between Bridge and Neck coils:

$

\Delta \phi = \omega \frac{\Delta x}{c_{\text{string}}}, \qquad c_{\text{string}} = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}

$

detects traveling wave energy, immediately breaking the $180^\circ$ upstream/downstream wind direction ambiguity.

Audio Sonification \& Ultrasonic Heterodyne Downconversion

For high-speed storms ($v > 25\text{ m/s}$) or ultra-fine wire gauges ($d < 0.25\text{ mm}$), the vortex shedding frequency enters the ultrasonic domain ($f_s > 20\text{ kHz}$):

$

f_s = \mathrm{St} \cdot \frac{v_\perp}{d} \approx 0.20 \cdot \frac{30\text{ m/s}}{0.00025\text{ m}} = 24,000\text{ Hz}

$

To enable acoustic monitoring and human sonification, we apply heterodyne frequency mixing:

$

s_{\text{audio}}(t) = \left[ s_{\text{HF}}(t) \cdot \cos(2\pi f_{\text{LO}} t) \right] \ast h_{\text{LPF}}(t)

$

mixing the $24\text{ kHz}$ ultrasonic vortex scream down to an audible $2.5\text{ kHz}$ baseband.

Dual-Modal Fusion: EM Pickups vs. Acoustic Microphones

Deploying an electromagnetic pickup alongside an external MEMS microphone provides powerful common-mode acoustic noise rejection:

  • Electromagnetic Pickup: Samples solely the string's mechanical velocity $\dot{y}(t)$. Completely immune to airborne acoustic noise (horns, sirens, traffic) because acoustic air pressure does not magnetize the coil.
  • Microphone: Captures ambient acoustic pressure $p_{\text{acoustic}}(t)$.
  • Coherence Fusion Filter: When cross-spectral coherence $\gamma^2(f) = \frac{|S_{EM, Mic}(f)|^2}{S_{EM}(f) S_{Mic}(f)} \approx 1.0$, the event is validated as a genuine fluid-dynamic vortex. External loud sirens ($\gamma^2 \to 0$ or low EM admittance) are filtered out immediately.

Psychrometric Humidity \& Air Density ($\rho_{\text{moist

}$) Calibration}

Humid air is less dense than dry air because water vapor ($M_{\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}} \approx 18\text{ g/mol}$) is lighter than diatomic nitrogen and oxygen ($M_{\text{dry}} \approx 28.97\text{ g/mol}$). The exact moist air density is given by the psychrometric formula:

$

\rho(T, p, \mathrm{RH}) = \frac{p_d}{R_d T} + \frac{p_v}{R_v T} = \frac{p - \phi p_{\text{sat}}(T)}{R_d T} + \frac{\phi p_{\text{sat}}(T)}{R_v T}

$

At $35^\circ\text{C}$ and $95\%$ relative humidity, air density decreases by nearly $2.0\%$, shifting dynamic lift forces $F_L = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 C_L$. Furthermore, near $100\%$ RH dew point condensation, water micro-droplets add mass $\Delta \mu$ to the wire, which our active EBow plink detects and recalibrates in real time.
