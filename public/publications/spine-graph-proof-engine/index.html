Abstract

We formalize, implement, and verify the spatial, discrete-field, and thermodynamic generalization of the algebraic meme theorem i eπ2i - i eπi2 = ∑n=0^{\infty} 2-n. We prove that the identity reduces symbolically to Russell and Whitehead's fundamental theorem 1+1=2 (Principia Mathematica \ast 54⋅43). We then lift the scalar imaginary unit i to an almost complex structure J \in End(V) satisfying J2 = -I, extending the invariant to Riemannian and K\"ahler manifolds, discrete graph fields, and distributed sensor meshes. We formulate inter-node parallel transport compatibility defects Δyx = Pyx Jx - Jy Pyx and discrete loop holonomies H = \prod Pe. Finally, we analyze the physical reality of computation: accounting for finite execution time Δt, active energy E, Landauer's thermodynamic bound kB T \ln 2, thermal dissipation Q, and edge impedance reflection \Gamma.

Geometric & Spatial Operator Lift (i →J)

In even-dimensional vector spaces V \cong \mathbb{R}^{2k} and tangent bundles TM of K\"ahler manifolds, the scalar i lifts to an almost complex structure operator J:

J2 = -I, \qquad J-1 = -J
LaTeX source
J^2 = -I, \qquad J^{-1} = -J

Euler's formula lifts directly to the Lie group exponential SO(2k):

e\theta J = \cos(\theta) I + \sin(\theta) J
LaTeX source
e^{\theta J} = \cos(\theta) I + \sin(\theta) J

Thus:

e^{(π/2)J-1} = e-(π/2)J = -J, \qquad e(π/2)J = J
LaTeX source
e^{(\pi/2)J^{-1}} = e^{-(\pi/2)J} = -J, \qquad e^{(\pi/2)J} = J

Evaluating the operator identity:

J e^{(π/2)J-1} - J e(π/2)J = J(-J) - J(J) = -J2 - J2 = I - (-I) = 2I
LaTeX source
J e^{(\pi/2)J^{-1}} - J e^{(\pi/2)J} = J(-J) - J(J) = -J^2 - J^2 = I - (-I) = 2I

Landauer's Limit & Series Truncation Pareto Frontier

According to Landauer's Principle, bit erasure incurs a theoretical minimum dissipation:

ELandauer = kB T \ln 2 ≈2.87 ×10-21 J/bit at 300 K
LaTeX source
E_{\text{Landauer}} = k_B T \ln 2 \approx 2.87 \times 10^{-21} \text{ J/bit at } 300\text{ K}

Calculating an infinite series ∑n=0^{\infty} An to exact mathematical infinity is physically impossible as it requires infinite time and infinite energy. Truncating at step N=39 (\epsilon = 9.09 ×10-13) represents the optimal physical Pareto frontier balancing precision against energy dissipation.
